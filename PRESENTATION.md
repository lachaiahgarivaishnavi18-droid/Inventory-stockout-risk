## 1-minute version
I built an inventory stockout risk prediction project to solve a common supply-chain problem. The business challenge is that products can run out of stock before the next replenishment arrives, creating lost sales and poor customer experience. I used a synthetic but realistic inventory dataset with sales, stock, lead time, promotions, and supplier information. After cleaning the data and handling missing values, I explored trends such as category-wise stockout rate, sales versus stock, and lead time versus risk. I created features like days_of_inventory, lead_time_demand, stock_gap, and sales_growth to explain inventory pressure. Then I trained a logistic regression baseline and a random forest model using a chronological split. I evaluated the models using precision, recall, F1-score, confusion matrix, and ROC-AUC, with recall being especially important because missing a risky item can be costly. The final system predicts risk and supports replenishment decisions for high-risk products.

## 2-minute version
The problem I solved is inventory stockout prediction. Stockout means a product is unavailable because demand may exceed available stock before the next order arrives. This is a business problem because it creates lost sales, lower service levels, and operational disruption. The objective of the project was to predict whether a product would be at high risk of stockout in the next 7 days.

I worked with a realistic synthetic dataset that included product ID, date, category, supplier, current stock, daily sales, reorder level, lead time, incoming stock, promotions, price, and holiday features. I first cleaned the data by removing duplicates, fixing missing values, standardizing categories, and checking invalid numbers like negative stock or negative sales. I then performed EDA to understand product distribution, category-wise risk, stockout patterns, and relationships between sales, stock, supplier lead time, and promotions.

For feature engineering, I created meaningful inventory features such as days_of_inventory, lead_time_demand, stock_gap, reorder_gap, sales_growth, and promotion_flag. These features help the model understand whether current stock is enough to cover near-term demand and supplier delay. I created the target variable stockout_risk using a business rule based on expected future demand and stock remaining after replenishment. I used a chronological train/test split to reduce leakage and trained a logistic regression baseline and a random forest classifier. I evaluated them using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, confusion matrix, and ROC-AUC.

The model helps the business prioritize replenishment for high-risk products and improves action speed. In simple terms, the project shows a full data science workflow: understanding the business problem, preparing the data, engineering features, training the model, evaluating it, and turning the result into a useful decision-support tool.
